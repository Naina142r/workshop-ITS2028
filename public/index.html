Welcome, workshop grower! Chinese chives (韭菜, Allium tuberosum) are a tough perennial: plant once, harvest for years. Track yours for a whole year, take on weekly challenges and swap tips with the community.

How to care for Chinese chives

☀️ Light

Full sun (6+ hours) is best, but they tolerate partial shade. Great for sunny windowsills and balconies.

🌡️ Temperature

Very hardy. Grow in spring through fall; blades die back in winter and the clump regrows in spring.

💧 Water

About 1 inch per week. Water when the top inch of soil is dry; avoid soggy soil, which rots the roots.

🌱 Sowing

Sow seeds ¼–½" deep (soak overnight to speed things up). They sprout in 7–14 days. Fresh seed works best. Thin to 6" apart.

🪴 Soil & Pots

Well-draining, rich soil, pH 6–7. Use a pot at least 8" deep with drainage holes. Add compost each spring.

✂️ Harvest

Be patient in year one: wait until blades are 6–8" tall. Cut 1–2" above the soil and they grow back in 2–3 weeks. Repeat all season.

🌸 Flowers

White star-shaped flowers appear in late summer. The buds are edible! Deadhead spent blooms so seeds don't spread everywhere.

🐛 Pests & Division

Watch for aphids, thrips and onion fly; remove yellow blades. Every 3–4 years, divide the clump to share with friends.

Your year with chives

Spring: sow or divide, feed with compost. Summer: start harvesting regularly. Late summer: enjoy flower buds, deadhead the rest. Fall: last big harvest. Winter: leaves die back; keep soil lightly moist and wait. Your chives return!
